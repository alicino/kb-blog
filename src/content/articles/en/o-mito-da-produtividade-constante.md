The idea that a good professional performs equally every day doesn't survive
two weeks of honest observation of your own routine.

## Energy is not linear

Bad sleep, an emotionally heavy day, or simply a natural burst of creativity
all change your output in ways that no productivity technique cancels out
completely.

## What actually helps

- Keep a list of "low energy" tasks for the bad days.
- Reserve the high-energy days for work that demands more focus.
- Measure progress in weeks, not in isolated days.

> Comparing your worst day of the week with someone else's best day is the
> perfect recipe for always feeling behind.

## Accepting variation is a skill

Just as you learn to plan, you also learn not to punish yourself on days with
lower output. And over time, that pays off more than forcing a constancy that
never truly existed.
