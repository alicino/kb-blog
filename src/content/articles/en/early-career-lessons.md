None of these lessons are original. But hearing someone say something doesn't
replace feeling it yourself, and that is why I write the way I wish I had read
it before.

## Asking early costs less than it seems

Spending two days stuck because you're embarrassed to ask something "obvious"
has always cost more than the question itself. Almost nobody judges a
well-formed question.

## Not every piece of criticism needs an immediate defense

Taking time to digest feedback before responding has avoided several replies I
would later regret.

## Document decisions, not just code

Six months later, the "why" behind a decision is worth more than the "how."
Comments and decision records save you from archaeology in the codebase.

> The code explains what the system does. It rarely explains why it exists the
> way it does.

## A career is not a linear race

Changing direction, slowing down for a while, or starting over in another field
is not a setback. It is just new information being incorporated into the
journey.
