There's a point where optimizing your routine stops helping and starts getting
in the way. It happens when timing your own rest ironically becomes one more
task.

## The most common symptom

Spending more time tweaking your productivity system than actually producing.
Switching task apps for the fourth time in a month is usually a sign of
procrastination disguised as organization.

## What has worked best

- A small number of commitments per day, not an endless list.
- Focus blocks without metrics of "how long I stayed focused."
- Accepting that some days will produce less, without needing to justify why.

### A simple example

Instead of tracking every minute, I use three questions at the end of the day:

1. What did I really need to do today?
2. Did it get done?
3. What will get stuck tomorrow if I don't resolve it now?

That replaces any elaborate productivity dashboard.

## Focus is not the absence of rest

Maybe the most counterintuitive idea is this one: resting on purpose, without
guilt, tends to yield more focus afterward than squeezing one more hour of
tired work.
